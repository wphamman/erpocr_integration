Can Star Pops' expense-account rules be learned from history?

Source: the Star Pops bench restore (OCR imports 2026-02-20 to 2026-06-09), read-only. Test month 2026-05-05 to 2026-06-04, the same as jev_lab eval #1. No person's name, vehicle registration or amount appears on this page.

Answer

Mostly yes, provided the rules keep learning from every posted invoice. Learned that way, simple rules code about two lines in three at roughly 94% accuracy. That is ready to review with the accountant.

The remaining third is mainly repair parts. Their account depends on which asset was repaired, and the invoice does not say. No rule can deduce that. It needs a fact from somewhere else.

Rules frozen on history before 5 May do poorly. That month's spending mix changed sharply, and a frozen rulebook cannot see accounts it has barely met.

The rule tiers, in order: a stock item always posts to Stock Received but not Billed (22 lines, 100% right). Next, a word that has always meant one account for that supplier (217 lines, 93.5%). Then a supplier that only ever uses one account (144 lines, 95.1%). Last, a word that means one account across several suppliers (26 lines, 69%), which is too weak to trust.

Why frozen rules fail this month. Travel Expenses – Local had 17 lines before 5 May and 201 in the test month. Entertainment went from 0 to 13. 36 of the month's 85 suppliers had never appeared before. A rulebook learned once and then frozen cannot predict that. One learning continuously picks it up within days.

jev_lab reported 52% for "the supplier's usual account". That figure does not reproduce here. Learning only from OCR invoices, up to each invoice, gives 77.8%. The difference is probably in how the history was cut, and jev_lab has been asked to reconcile it.

ITEM001 and SER001: practice against policy

The stated policy is ITEM001 for non-stock goods and SER001 for services. In practice nearly everything is ITEM001. 230 lines worded as a service (labour, hire, rental, subscription, fees, call-outs, repairs, transport) all went on ITEM001. SER001 appears on only 8 lines, all subcontracted transport.

This does not affect which expense account a line lands on, because the account is chosen per line. It does matter for any report that splits goods from services, and for any future rule keyed on the item. Decide which is right: enforce the policy from a date, or retire SER001 and say so.

What this means for auto-draft

Today erpocr learns an expense account only for the exact wording of a confirmed line. That is why it reaches just 8% of lines. The rules above generalise across wording and still learn continuously, and they would code two lines in three.

This page proposes nothing to build. Once the accountant has confirmed or corrected the rules, any change to erpocr's matching gets its own plan and review.

Method and limits

  • Lines: every line of a submitted purchase invoice created from an OCR import. The test month has 590 such lines. jev_lab's eval uses 534, because it keeps only lines it could pair with the OCR text.
  • Extra history: 4,797 non-OCR purchase-invoice lines posted before the test month. They barely change the result.
  • Rules: a word, or a supplier and word, qualifies when at least 90% of its history went to one account, with at least two or three lines behind it. Words are three letters or more, with common words removed.
  • Privacy: all line-level data stayed inside the bench container. Only counts, account names, company names and product words left it.
  • Limits: Star Pops only, one test month. Account splits between repair families are judged by account group, so a few "needs a fact" lines may really be slips.
